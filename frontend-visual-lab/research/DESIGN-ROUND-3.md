# Round three: design plan

## Honest critique of round two

The verdict, concept by concept:

- **Inspect and Assurance** fit Numek best.
- **Continual's hero** sits too close to Wafer.
- **Signal** is close to the dark-SaaS template.
- **Argument's app** is a reading room, not a working surface.

Three weaknesses ran across all five:

- **App screens stayed shallow.** They showed four findings and five bars. None let you browse the dataset itself, and that is where an ML engineer actually spends time.
- **No concept had a full brand system.** A mark, motion signature and type system did not carry from the landing page into the app.
- **Hero visuals mostly illustrated a score.** They did not demonstrate the product's core act, which is tracing a risk back to its rows.

## The bar for round three

1. **The hero demonstrates the core act truthfully.** It traces, measures or explains real Adult evidence, using the real data.
2. **The app is data-native.** Real distributions for all 15 columns from the pinned CSV (`research/extract_distributions.py`) appear in the app as column profiles. The engine's methods sit beside them.
3. **One brand system runs through each concept.** A mark, tokens, type and a single motion signature, used consistently on all three screens.
4. **Craft is taken from the references, never subject matter.** Every reference's lesson is stated as a technique.

## Reviewing the plan against generic defaults

- **Proof:** a white page with an indigo gradient leans on Stripe's identity. The gradient is used only as a moving mesh behind product cards, and the coral end tilts it toward Numek. The calculator is the real differentiator.
- **Engine:** near-black with one bright accent is a known tell. Graphite and bone on red is warmer, and the identity is the exploded drawing, not the colour.
- **Notebook:** cream paper with a serif is a known tell. The grid texture, real code cells and sage-and-ember chips come from notebook vocabulary, not decoration.
