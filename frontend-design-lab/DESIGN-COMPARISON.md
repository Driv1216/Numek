# Five distinct ways of working

The same public Adult classification evidence and synthetic M9 collection make the core tasks comparable. This is product exploration, not a production recommendation.

| Dimension | Guided Studio | Analyst Console | Evidence Workbench | Decision Edition | Analytical Studio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary information architecture | Purpose → inputs → declarations → confirmation → investigation | Searchable finding/record queue | Open investigations and source work areas | Conclusion → evidence → methods → artifacts | Population → chart selection → linked evidence |

## Critical comparison

Guided Studio makes task meaning explicit and contextual. It is strongest when the user is new to a family or faces an unfamiliar declaration. The procedural sequence can feel restrictive to an experienced investigator. It needs meaningful shortcuts if developed further.

Analyst Console makes movement among findings cheap. Search, sorting, filtering, keyboard selection and a command palette work. Four classification findings naturally leave spare queue space; the design must not invent findings to look busy. Its density is a professional tradeoff, and prolonged readability would benefit from real-user typography trials.

Evidence Workbench gives the source surface priority and keeps rule context nearby. Its pane width, stacked layout, inspector visibility and source representation are adjustable. It is the most promising exploration for source-heavy investigations, but a general text/image/DOCX workbench would require further modality-specific viewers and real task validation. The current implementation demonstrates the shared native-PDF scenario only.

Decision Edition connects executive conclusions to exact evidence through reading structure. It explains the difference between discrepancies and competing sources without turning them into a single alarm. It is best suited to report review and knowledge transfer. It is less efficient for repeated record-by-record review, and should preserve direct evidence links if developed further.

Analytical Studio turns selection into investigation. Its chart/table pairs, categorical revenue comparison and measured hours bins are grounded in actual fixtures. It makes scope visible without implying a statistical relationship. It must resist visualizing checks that lack appropriate measurements; its current scope is descriptive, not an inferential analysis tool.

## Structural differentiation check

Removing the palettes and fonts leaves a stage workspace, a queue, a source canvas, an article and a plot workspace. Console and Workbench both use contextual inspectors, but the working content differs: selecting a finding row versus manipulating record/source panes. Guided and Edition both expand technical detail, but their sequencing differs: completing declarations versus following an argument. Shared fixtures and download helpers do not impose a common application shell.

The choice should remain open until users compare setup friction, triage speed, evidence comprehension, sustained readability and report usefulness. No concept is declared the winner.
